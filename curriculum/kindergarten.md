# SDOC Elementary Music Curriculum: Kindergarten

Course Elementary Music - Kindergarten
Unit Q1 Unit 1: Welcome to Music!
Standards/
Benchmarks
MU.K.F.3.1: Exhibit age-appropriate music and life skills that will add to the success in the music 
classroom
MU.K.S.3.2: Perform simple songs and accompaniments
MU.K.C.1.1: Respond to music from various sound sources to show awareness of steady beat.
Concepts/Content
• Introduce students to the music classroom
• Establish rules, procedures, and personal space
• Sing simple hello songs and play name games to build community
• Reinforce keeping a steady beat.
Pacing Q1 / Rotation 1
Learning Goals – “Can Do” Statements Learning Targets – “I Will” Statements
I can…
• I can be a respectful member of our music class
• I can learn my friends' names
• I can feel the steady beat.
I will...
• I will listen when my teacher is talking
• I will sing my name when it is my turn
• I will clap and move to a steady beat.

Vocabulary • Beat; No Beat; Pitched; Unpitched; Loud; Quiet; Body Percussion
Assessment Strategies
Formative Assessment/Progress Monitoring Summative Assessment

Unit Q1 Unit 2: The Beat is All Around
Standards/
Benchmarks
MU.K.C.1.1: Respond to music from various sound sources to show awareness of steady beat
MU.K.S.3.1: Sing songs of limited range appropriate to the young child and use the head voice
MU.K.O.1.1: Respond to beat, rhythm, and melodic line through imitation.
Concepts/Content
• Discover the presence of a steady beat in music and the world
• Feel and express a steady beat through locomotor movements like marching and 
walking
• Differentiate beat vs. no beat.
Pacing Q1 / Rotation 2
Learning Goals – “Can Do” Statements Learning Targets – “I Will” Statements
I can…
• I can feel the beat in music
• I can move my body to the steady beat.
I will...
• I will march around the room to the beat of a drum
• I will stop moving when the music stops
• I will identify if a piece of music has a steady beat or 
no beat.
Language Goal Frame
Students will (learning target/ standard) orally and/or in writing by using (with the aid of) (tool 
for comprehensible input) to complete (create) a (product to demonstrate learning/outputbased on language function) with the support of Choose an item. (Select an additional support if 
needed)
Vocabulary • Steady Beat (beat vs no beat); Head Voice; Echo; Rhythm
Assessment Strategies
Formative Assessment/Progress Monitoring Summative Assessment
• Observe each student to see if they can maintain the 
steady beat
• Provide immediate feedback
• Check for consistency and accuracy in maintaining 
the beat.
• Student successfully marches to the steady beat of a 
new piece of music for 16 counts without stopping.
Life Skills Competencies Focus: Maintaining a steady motor pattern over time
Body Awareness: Coordinating physical movement with an external auditory pulse.
Resources & Examples Quaver: LP 2 Beat; GamePlan K: August Week 2: Goin’ to the Farm; Listen to the Hens; Clap Your

Unit Q1 Unit 3: Fast & Slow Beats
Standards/
Benchmarks
MU.K.C.1.1: Respond to music from various sound sources to show awareness of steady beat
MU.K.S.3.1: Sing songs of limited range appropriate to the young child and use the head voice
MU.K.O.1.1: Respond to beat, rhythm, and melodic line through imitation.
Concepts/Content • Introduce tempo as the speed of the music
• Explore the musical opposites of fast and slow through listening, singing, and moving
• Maintain a steady beat at different tempos.
Pacing Q1 / Rotation 3
Learning Goals – “Can Do” Statements Learning Targets – “I Will” Statements
I can…
• I can show if music is fast or slow
• I can keep a steady beat at different speeds.
I will...
• I will move quickly like a rabbit when the music is fast
• I will move slowly like a turtle when the music is slow
• I will practice singing with my head voice keeping a 
steady beat.
Language Goal Frame
Students will (learning target/ standard) orally and/or in writing by using (with the aid of) (tool 
for comprehensible input) to complete (create) a (product to demonstrate learning/outputbased on language function) with the support of Choose an item. (Select an additional support if 
needed)
Vocabulary • Tempo; Fast; Slow
Assessment Strategies
Formative Assessment/Progress Monitoring Summative Assessment
• Students use scarves to show fast and slow tempos in 
listening examples
• Students use responder cards (e.g., a picture of a 
rabbit, a picture of a turtle) to identify the tempo of 
musical excerpts.
• Given two contrasting musical examples, the student 
correctly identifies which is fast and which is slow 
through movement or verbal response.
Life Skills Competencies Self-Control: Adjusting movement speed to match the music
Analytical Skills: Listening for and identifying the speed of the beat.
Resources & Examples
Quaver: LP 3 Steady Beat; GamePlan K: September Week 4: Hey Betty Martin; Listen to the Cows; 
Songs: "March of the Ducks," "Scarves in the Wind"; Listening: "Flight of the Bumblebee" (fast), 
"The Swan" (slow).
