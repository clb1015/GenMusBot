1. My underdog is overweight, by: Jack frelutskyi It's 
Raining Pigs and Noodles pg. 6 
FOCUS: fermata and pair of eighth notes 
a. Read the poem and find words that repeat, find the 
rhythm to those words and practice. 
b. Ask students "what is that thing over "bear"? (fermata) 
discuss and practice 
c. Add BP to reoccurring words, under (pat), over (clap). 
Perform 
d. Split class and add NPP to UNDER and OVER in two 
groups. Switch instruments. Don't forget to play the 
fermata on BEAR!

3. Witches Menu, by: Sonja Nikolay/ The Random 
House Book of Poetry for Children pg. 203 (I left 
out the last paragraph of this poem to fit with the solfege 
scale) 
fOCUS: playing and singing the solfege scale 
a. Begin by reading the poem to the class in your speaking 
voice. Discuss with your class if they would want this to 
be their menu! EWWWW! 0; 
b. Then display the poem with the rhythm. Ask students to 
say the poem this time while clapping the rhythm. Look 
for similarities in the phrases. You may choose to let your 
students use a NPP instrument with the rhythm at this point 
c. Ask the students then "How many phrases do you see?" and 
relate that number (8) to the number of solfege syllables 
we have in our scale 
d. Then sing the poem for your students using each solfege 
syllable for each phrase. I also like my students to sing 
the solfege syllables a few times with the rhythm as well. 
e. Transfer playing the melody of the scale to the Orff 
instruments. My students en joy putting a ratchet sound on 
the rests or sometimes we have thought of "gross' words 
to say On the rests (yuck, Ewww, BLEW, etc.) 
4. I Raised a Great Hullabaloo, by: Anonymous/ The 
Random House Book of Poetry for Children pg. 150 
fOCUS: playing and singing the solfege scale, octave 
a. I begin this poem by singing it on the melody and 
performing with hand signs and ask my kids to have 
something intelligent to say when I'm done! 
b. Ask kids to join in with you and focus on the hand signs 
in the two phrases that have the octave. 
C. I focus all of my children on the melody at first and then 
break them up into groups to perform the accompaniment 
parts. You might want to do this activity over two class 
periods. (see notation in the back) 5. Homework! Oh itomework, by: Jack fretutsky/ The 
New Kid on the Block pg. 54 
FOCUS: legato and staccato, microbeat vs. macrobeat 
a. Teacher sings the song for the students with the staccato 
and legato parts and discuss. 
b. Class learns song and then focuses on the macrobeat and 
microbeat as the ostinatos. 
c. Students will pat the microbeat on the staccato part and 
then pat the macrobeat on the legato part. 
d. Transfer to orff in C pentatonic and improvise with macro 
and micro beat.

8. the Last Cry of the Damp fly, by: Dennis Lee/ 
Laughing out Loud pg. 63 
fOCUS: ascending/descending, S/f/M/R/D 
a. Teacher sings song for students and discusses the story 
behind the poem 
b. If used in younger grades we decide what phrases are 
ascending and which are descending. If used with older 
grades we find the solfege pattern used and sing with hand 
signs 
c. for fun and good practice we change the first letter of the 
"Bitter Batter..." phrases, for example, "Mitter Matter Moop." 
This gives the kids some great practice and some fun . . 
Someone will eventually pick the letter "P." 
d. Younger Grades: We set up in C pentatonic and discuss that 
we have LOW bars (BIG) and 14IG44 bars (SMALL). Students 
play 2 44IG44 bars on the 14IG44 rhyming word, then 2 LOW 
bars on the LOW rhyming word. for example: BOOP-2 141G14 
bars, SOUP-2 LOW bars. 
e. Older Grades: We play the melody of S/f/M/R/D, as we sing 
the song. 9. It is foolish to Relax, by: Jack Prelutsky/ It's 
Raining Pigs and Noodles pg. 95 
fOCUS: dynamics, triplet 
a. speak poem for children and ask them what the reoccurring 
pattern is (clickety-clack). Discover the rhythm that goes 
along with that pattern. 
b. Have children speak the pattern and you speak the rest of 
the poem. Talk with children about when they hear a 
train coming from far away and how the volume of the 
train gets louder as it gets closer. 
c. Perform with dynamic changes in the poem using rhythm 
sticks, throw a train whistle or two in there as well for 
fun! 
BOOKS: 
1. Up, Down, and Around/ By: Katherine Ayres 
fOCUS: melodic direction, glissandos, improvisation 
a. I like to pair this book with the song Oats, Peas, Beans 
and Barley Grow, since the book is about growing food. 
b. We learn the song by echo singing and then take out our 
pointing pages and focus on the melodic direction with our 
pointers. 
c. Then we have a small discussion that there are directions 
on our Orff instruments that let us know where the high 
and low notes are. I demonstrate UP and DOWN glissando 
and have them pantomime their UP and DOWN. 
d. We then discuss IMPROVISATION and how we get to choose 
the notes that we play. 
e. I read the book and have students pantomime UP gliss. 
when I say UP, Down gliss. when I say DOWN, and IMPROV 
when I say AROUND AND AROUND. 
f. I remind students that they need to watch the conductor 
because when I turn the page they must go to silent 
position for the next page. 
2. The Pout-Pout fish/The Pout-Pout fish and the BigBig Dark By: Deborah Diesen 
fOCUS: quarter, half and whole notes OR major vs. minor Quarter/fialf/Whole: 
a. Teach students the refrain for Pout Pout fish. 
b. You will want to read this book with a rhythm and 
the stanza before the refrain begins make sure you 
have a rubato so that the students come in at the 
right time. 
c. At the BLOB parts students will sing the first bulb on 
middle C first on a quarter note, then half, then 
whole note. I like to count on my fingers for them 
to see the first few times before they get it in their 
head. 
d. Have students pat their laps to a tremolo on one leg 
on the first read to prepare for Orff instruments. 
e. On the second read students are at the Orff 
instruments and play a closed bordun during the 
refrain and the tremolo on Low C for the BLOB 
section. 
Major vs. Minor: 
a. for the major vs. minor lesson I teach the original 
Pout Pout fish in major key and then the next week I 
teach the same melody but in minor mode with the 
different words for the "Big-Fig Dark" version. 
b. Another option idea I got from Cindy Krulick, was for 
the original Pout Pout fish book. She suggested 
putting the refrain in the minor key until the Pout 
Pout fish turns his frown upside down and then the 
refrain will change to major. 
3. The Sun is my favorite star/ By: frank Asch 
focus: melodic direction, crescendo, decresendo 
a. Teach students the refrain in whichever pattern you decide 
to start with (ascending or descending the solfege ladder, 
DO to DO) 
As \.s ),\." b. Read the book once and have students sing the refrain 
when it appears. 
a. Ascending - students sing the refrain as a crescendo 
while bringing their arms up to the sky and ending in 
a rainbow effect (a sunrise). 
\\ b. Descending - students sing the refrain as a decresendo 
while bringing their arms from the rainbow effect, 
down to their laps (a sunset). c. The next class period I usually read the book once more 
and have students go back and forth between the ascending 
and descending pattern. 
d. This book is a great beginning to "The Sun is Rising" lesson 
or "Twinkle, Twinkle Little Star" lesson in Get to the Point! 
4. The Straight Line Wonder/ By: Mem fox 
fOCUS: Borduns, accompaniments, minor scale 
a. There are 5 reoccuring refrains in this book. Each 
refrain is assigned a bordun and each bordun is played 
by a specific Orff instrument. 
b. Students will play their specific bordun when the refrain 
is introduced in the book and then every other time it is 
mentioned. 
c. All instruments will play during the refrains "People will 
stare" and "Stay Straight Silly!" 
5. Leap Back Home to Me/ By: Lauren Thompson 
fOCUS: Alternating Hands and Hands Together 
a. Place Orff instruments in a pentatonic scale. You can 
decide to focus on either AR or HT on the reoccurring "Leap 
frog" in the book. Allow students to play either HT on 2 notes 
of their choosing, or A+4 with one note on each word. 
b. You can also incorporate both Aft and HT with the book. 
Have your students play HT on the "Leap frog" refrain and then 
use A14 on the refrain "Leap Back Home to Me." Soprano
