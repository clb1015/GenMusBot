This hour's examples focus on D Major pentatonic (remove C's and G's, and replace F's 
with F # 's). This is transposed pentatonic and used because it fits the range of the 
child's voice and the availability ofF #'son the instruments. 
After experiencing melodies with 2, 3, and 4 notes, introduce the full pentatonic (5-note) 
scale. In this melody there are no half steps, D-R-M-S-L. 
Advantages of the pentatonic: 
· I. No harmonic changes. 
2. Pentatonic melodies may be accompanied with a bo~dun. 
3. With no dissonance, all notes r;nay sound together providing opportunities for 
creativitY and improvisation. . ' 
4. Cultures throughout the world have a rich heritage oc'pentatonic melodies, 
On the barred instruments, there are three true major pentatonic scales: 
C Ma~or/a minor F Major/d minor G Majo('/e minor 
Because of the accidentals available on the instruments, and the range of the child's voice, it is. 
possible to work in the D Major pentatonic (with the addition of F'#'s).
