# Hemond Orff Presentation

New Tricks for Old Treasures:
how to add Music Learning Theory into your Orff classroom
presented by Erin Hemond
What Is Music Learning Theory?
Music Learning Theory is a body of research on how we learn music. Some of the 
key concepts include AUDIATION (hearing and understanding music internally) 
and teaching music in a certain sequence.
Official Music Learning Theory Website: www.giml.org
Focuses on:
-­‐ sequence of learning
-­‐ whole-part-whole
Activity:
Verse 1 – walk to the beat
Verse 2 – do motions in place.
-­‐ sow the seed: pretend to plant seeds
-­‐ takes his ease: hands on hips
-­‐ stomp his foot: STOMP/ claps his hands: CLAP
-­‐ turns around to view his lands: spin around once.Verse 3 – find a partner before the end of the verse (This part CHANGES when 
we go into note reading. I will sing “looking at the board now” and students 
freeze and turn to face the board to read the rhythm.)
Levels of Learning:
1. Aural/Oral – students clap hands with a partner on a neutral syllable 
(echoing the teacher)
2. Verbal Association – add syllables to rhythms
3. Partial Synthesis – SWITCH BETWEEN METERS. students identify meter
4. Symbolic Association – echo-read rhythms/read rhythms
5. Composite Synthesis – SWITCH BETWEEN METERS. Hold up written rhythms.
Students identify as duple/triple meter.
ADD ONS: improvisation/rhythmic conversations/students create rhythm during 
song for class to read
Pitch Song
Focuses on:
-­‐ preparatory sequence
-­‐ introduction of PITCH (singing V-I)
-­‐ exposure to “exotic” modes
-­‐ audiation
-­‐ modeling continuous fluid movement
Activity: Teacher improvises a melody while holding a ball with the word PITCH 
on it (idea from Brian Burnett – realia). Sing V-I as you toss the ball. Have students 
sing V-I as you toss the ball. Define what the word PITCH means. Give students 
turns to toss the ball to others in the circle.
ADD-ONS: have students audiate instead of singing/have individual students 
sing as you toss the ball to them
