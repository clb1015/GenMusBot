1. Finale from William Tell Overture (Rossini): Non-Pitched Percussion
Focus: Form (Introduction/AB/nterlude/ACDCB/interlude/A/Coda), Steady Beat, Timbre.
From Parachutes, Ribbons and Scarves, Oh My! by Almeida.
Explain to the children that they are the non-pitched percussion ensemble and they will perform today under
the baton of a very famous conductor — YOU! Divide children into three groups. | use hulahoops as station
organizers. Assign A, B, C Sections to groups of children. | use Jingle Bells for the A Section, Maracas for B
and Hand Drums for C. D is silent. All instruments play the Coda. Give tips for correct playing technique.
Conduct with great style and fanfare. ©
2. Hoop Groups (Download the PPT with directions from my website: www.artiealmeida.com)
Focus: Timbre, Steady Beat, Rhythm Patterns. (The music | used is called “Wildwood Flower” by Hap
Palmer, from the album Homemade Band. Although the correct name is “Wildwood Flower, on iTunes it is
called “Wild Flower’). Prepare buckets of non-pitched percussion for four groups: Woods, Metals, Shakers,
Membranes (or Skins). Place some ordinary and some exotics of each group into the buckets and line the
buckets up in a row — separated from one another — with a
hula hoop around each bucket to define the space. Quickly demonstrate the proper technique for each
instrument. Display PPT and discuss directions with children. Divide into four equal groups and seat children
around the hoops. Each child chooses an instrument, the teacher starts the music and the activity begins. At
the end of the piece, children place their instruments inside the hoop, but not in the buckets! Children move
to the next timbre station and repeat the lesson. Do this four times, so all children play all timbres.
3. Percussion Song (Lesson by Lynn Kleiner, used with permission. Extended: Almeida),
Focus: Timbre, Steady Beat, Rhythm. Download PPTs from my website. (This great piece is from Kids
Make Music: Babies Make Music Too!” Even though the intended audience is preschool, a lot of these
lessons work very well with my public school kindergartners and first graders!) Use your buckets of
interesting percussion instruments from the lesson above, or simply use one type of instrument in each
bucket (e.g., all sticks in the Woods bucket, all tambourines in the Metals bucket, etc.) | frontload this melody,
teaching the song a week prior to the lesson. (1) Assign students to timbre groups and allow warm-up time,
while you check for proper technique. (2) WOODS go first, while other groups lay their instruments down in
front of them and sing along with the Woods. 1* Verse: Students play beat while singing. 2" verse: Play
rhythm while singing. 3 verse: Students improvise while teacher plays accompaniment. Notation will be
provided.
4, Book: Mama Built a Little Nest — book by Jennifer Ward (Pair this activity with #5: Little Bird)
(This nonfiction book pairs well with the Little Bird activity) (Download notation from my website)
Focus: Melody. For each page, teacher sings each verse, children respond with refrain.
5. Little Bird (Lesson idea by Kriske & Delles, used with permission. Modified Almeida)
Focus: AB Form, Timbre, Instrument Technique. Prepare the room with birds’ nests in a circle, with an
interesting percussion instrument “in” each nest. | use small hand percussion as well as larger things like
bongos, temple blocks, cajon drums, log drums, etc. A section: Children sing song, while seated at nests,
playing three quarter notes at end of each phrase, B Section: they place instruments back in nests, stand
and “Shake it to the east, shake it to the west, shake it on over ‘til you find another nest!” On the word “nes”
they jump to the next nest on their right and begin warming up the new instrument. | accompany this on
guitar, although the B Section is also lots of fun with a suspended cymbal playing a swing rhythm.
6. Popcorn — Kickball Routine (Download PPT from my website)
Focus: Note Values, AABA Form. Practice each section. Discuss repeated patterns. Emphasize safety
with the kickballs, not throwing above head due to light fixtures, etc. | downloaded the piece from iTunes.
Crazy Frog does a very fun version, but the form is a little less musically interesting (extended Introduction,
interludes, No B Section, etc).

1. The Wild Horseman (Schumann)—A Stick-Horse Adventure
Focus: AABA Form. From Parachutes, Ribbons and Scarves by Aimeida
Process: Use horsey finger puppets to demo first time. Divide into two groups (boys/girls). Establish a barn
on each side of room. One group rides for A, other B. Trade jobs. The last time, all chiidren ride on each A
and let horses graze on B.
2. William Tell Overture (Rossini) — Parachute Play
Focus: Form, Steady Beat. From Parachutes, Ribbons and Scarves by Almeida
A section: Trot in circle counterclockwise to beat
B section: Ripples and waves (shakes)
C section: Mushrooms (2) up and down to match the music
D section: “Washing Machine” (move back & forth, left then right)
CODA: Under teacher's direction, ripples and waves, toss at the end

1. Crazy Frog Finger Busters (based on idea by Tami Mangusso)
(1) Tap fingers against each other one at a time, then two, different combos, 1-2-3-4, etc.
(2) Finger Push-Ups
(3) At Interlude: Shake ‘em out!
(4) Steeples — one finger at a time
(5) Pop fingers on and off of finger hole: 1, 1-2, 1-2-3, Thumb, then call name B-A-G-A-B
(6) Shake out and Flick!
2. Recorder Simon (G, GA, GAG, GAGB, GAGBB, etc)
3. Recorder Relay
Three students are each assigned one note in a song (e.g., child one plays all the Bs, child two plays all the
As, child three plays all the Bs). Perform songs while maintaining a steady beat, attempting to match tones
as well. Gradually add all students in class to relay. Songs to consider: Hot Cross Buns, Buns Cross Hot,
Mary’s Lamb, Mary's Busy Lamb. Line up note signs G, A, B, C, D and do dictation. Add Low E.
4. Recorder Hike
Use rhythm track. Children step off on a 4-beat whistle count. March 16 beats and freeze, facing teacher.
Three levels of tasks cards: Letter names, notes on staff, easy songs, “Sit Down.”
5. Buns Bonanza
Divide children into four groups, standing on each side of room facing teacher in middle. Group 1
plays Hot Cross Buns, Group 2 plays Buns Cross Hot, Group 3 plays Killer Buns (D1-D-G, D1-D-
G, D-C-B-A-G-A-B-C-D1-D-G), Group 4 plays Bass Line (G-D-G, G-D-G, G-G-G-G, D-D-D-D, G-
D-G). Hold fermata at end. Give a 4-beat whistle count-off and children march to next stations,
changing parts. Continue until all students have played all four parts. Invite admin in for a concert!

7. Study Buddies - To emphasize importance of fingers and tongue working together.
Kids pair up. Child on right of pair grasps recorder by the foot join with their right hand and puts left hand
behind back. And says “I am the Articulator!” Child on left of pair puts right hand behind back and reaches
left hand over to do the fingerings on their partner’s recorder! They say “I am the Fingering Specialist!”
Perform a few tunes then change jobs. Consider circle formation for grand finale. ©

A selection of lessons from Mallet Madness & Mallet Madness Strikes Again by Almeida
1. Hands Together/Alternating (steady beat) (Set instruments in pentatonic mode)
Rotate students to next instrument using the poem below Rotate students through 5 or 6 different
instruments in a 10-minute time segment.
2. Rotation Poem:
1-2-3-4, Mallets down, get off the floor.
5-6-7-8, Hurry, don’t be late!
3._Crazy 8s (Pentatonic) .
Play a series of 8-beat phrases, first playing only on beat 1, then beats 1 and 2, etc., adding until all 8 beats
in the phrase are played. Reverse the process, playing all 8 beats, then the first 7, then 6, etc. down to 1.
4, Notes and Rests (Pentatonic)
Using note value flash cards for visual support, cue quarter notes, then move on to other values, always
putting quarter notes between the new values called.
5. Here’s a Quarter Note (Pentatonic)
| initially teach this activity with clapping, then transfer it to non-pitched percussion. The final transfer is to
barred instruments and drums. Display the note values on which you will focus. | use whole, half, quarter,
eighths, triplet and sixteenths. Review the value of each with the students. Begin playing quarter notes on a
woodblock or temple blocks, and rhythmically speak the sentence “Here are quarter notes, you play eighth
notes.” Students will play the eighths on any pitches they wish, against your quarters. Continue in this
fashion until all values have been experienced. Consider having the drummers keep the quarter beat with
the teacher. Assign different note values to different timbres, to be played at the same time.
6. Jack and Jill Rhythm Sequence (Pentatonic) Download rhythm visual from my website
Display visual of 4 rhythm patterns. Speak poem with a swing feel, playing two quarter notes at the end of
each measure. Then ti-ti-ta, then triplet-ta, then ticka-tcka-ta. Each time the pattern changes, draw students
attention to the subdivision details. In a future lesson, move the patterns up the mallet instruments for four
measures then down for four measures, as indicated in the lyrics.
7. Pease Porridge Hot
Focus: Timbre. 1. Chant poem, click mallets on rests (filling a silent beat with a sound is a Dalcroze
audiation technique called a “complementary rhythm.” 2. Chant poem while clicking notes with mallets
(clapping if you are a drummer) and playing the rests on the instruments. 3. Put notes on instruments and
click on rests. 4. Play notes on the instruments and leave the resis silent.
8. Melody Direction Activity: Up and Down in Mallet Town
(A) Walk my mallets to the top (match rhythm upward)
To the bottom, do not stop (match rhythm downwards)
Tap them in the middle, please,
Tap them on our home tone C.
Add a challenge by increasing the tempo. (B) Consider high/low echoes for a B Section.

2. One to Four (aka Quadraboom!) (C. Judah-Lauder in To Drum. Used with permission)
(For four graduated sizes of drums. | use the round Remo Shapes drums) (The C part was added by
Almeida) Focus: Form-ABCA. For the C Section = Click 7 sets of eighth notes/then a quarter note- on shell
while raising drum up in front of you. Take all 8 beats to get to top. (When practicing we say “1-and-2-and-3-
and-4-and-5-and-6-and-boom-boom-boom!”) Same thing on way back down. My students call this
QUADRABOOM! (Notation at end of handout)

4, March from The Nutcracker (Tchaikovsky): Plastic Plates
Activity from Parachutes, Ribbons and Scarves, Oh My! by Almeida.
A Sections: All Plates
Because there is no introduction in this piece, we listen to the first two measures of the A section, to hear
beat before we begin our plate movements. Hold plates with fingers dipping into the inside of the plate,
thumb against the back (students will be clapping the backs of the plates together). The pattern is Pat Lap-
Clap Plates Together-Hold Out to Sides by Face-Clap Plates Together (four times), then waving plates from
right to left to a half-note beat, which changes to a quarter-note beat to match the music (this works out to be
4 half-note waves and 7 qu.-note waves). Repeat both parts.
B Section: Red Plates Only (Risers on Left)
Students do 8 Taco Claps, which involves patting the bottom of plates together, alternating with right plate
on top, then left, etc., like patting out a tortilla. Next pat alternating 8th notes on their lap. Repeat both parts.
C Section: White Plates Only (Center Risers)
The children call this first motion the “Flying Dots.” Students wave the plates upward in a wiggly motion
for 4 beats, then back down in the same fashion. Then they fan themselves with the plates while leaning
to their right for four beats, then their left for four beats. Then both parts are repeatedSecond B Section:
Green Plates Only (Risers on Right)
Children do Brushes, brushing the bottoms of the plates together in large, brushing motions for 8 beats, then
do an 8-beat drumroll on the lap with the plates.
All Plates Repeat the A Section (with a slight change)
Extend the plates up above the head (with arms in a “V") instead of holding them out to the sides of the face,
so the pattern becomes Lap-Clap-UP-Clap for four repetitions. On the last section of the half note/quarter
note waves, change the last four beats of the piece to CLAP-CLAP-CLAP-UP! The plates should end above
the head in a “V’, freezing on the final note.
We like to put both plates together and “tip our hats” to the audience at the end.

About the Clinician
Dr. Artie Almeida is enjoying her 37" year of teaching and is the music specialist at Bear Lake Elementary
school in the Orlando FL area, where she teaches 1100 K-5 students. Her dynamic performing groups have
performed for NAfME, AOSA, and on the NBC Today Show. Look for The Bear Lake Sound in the upcoming
music education advocacy documentary “Marching Beyond Halftime.”
Artie was chosen as Florida Music Educator of the Year, and was also selected as an Intemational Educator
2006 by the Cambridge England Biographical Society. She was a Teacher of the Year at the school level 6
times and was recently chosen as a University of Central Florida Alumni of the Decade. She has presented
music education clinics in 42 states, four countries, and was the guest instructor at The American School in
Singapore in March of 2014. She is looking forward to teaching in Shanghai for the East Asian Council of
Schools in March of 2016.
In addition to her public schoo! teaching duties, Artie is an adjunct professor at the University of Central
Florida, teaches applied saxophone lessons and performs on historical winds with The Halifax Consort, an
early music ensemble.

KidStix Directions: (Tchaikovsky) March from the Nutcracker
Focus: Rondo Form, Rhythm Patterns.
No score — just teach by rote!
Each student needs sticks, beatboard or book, a tambourine and a
crash can. A Section = play rhythm on beatboard, clicking sticks on
rests. Change to tambourine on dotted eighth/sixteenth pattern. B
Section = Play two measures of clicked (against each other) quarter
notes, then two measures of clicked eighth notes. Repeat. C Section
= Roll on plexiglass. Consider replacing the stick clicks in B section
with quarters and eighths played on Pop Toobs. (Pop Toobs are
available from Amazon or West Music)
