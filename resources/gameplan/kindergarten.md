GAMEP'LAN is an active music curriculum for children. Organized by grade level and 
presented chronologically in weekly lessons, GAIVIEPtAN sequences musical skills and 
objectives that support the National Standards for Arts Education. As good teaching is 
diverse, this curriculum is eclectic. It embraces several philosophies, including the 
principles of Orff Schulwerk and the Kodaly approach to reading rhythm and melody. Foll 
songs, folk rhymes, and traditional games and dances form an integral part of the repertoin 
included. 
GAMERAN has two goals: 
• to organize musical objectives in a logical progression that is educationally 
sound and developmentally appropriate 
• to present the lessons in a concise format that is practical, easy to follow, and 
user-friendly 
GAMERAN parallels the school year from September through May and includes one 
lesson per week with approximately 60 minutes of activities. This is an active music 
curriculum. There are no student textbooks. All charts and visuals appear in the 
Appendices, and any additional materials required are listed on page x. 
Writing the Kindergarten component to GAIVIEPtAN posed a unique challenge for us. 
There are fewer objectives at this grade level and they are less sequenced than in 
subsequent grades, yet kindergarten students require lots of variety with numerous 
activities in each lesson. So the question became how to organize the material and keep 
it interesting. Our solution was to focus on a theme for each lesson. This creates variety 
from week to week while reinforcing the same basic skills. In addition, many of the 
themes reinforce kindergarten classroom objectives such as identifying numbers, 
recognizing colors, reinforcing the alphabet, etc. 
The following pages define and outline the kindergarten component of this curriculum. 
Take time to familiarize yourself with this information, as it explains how and why the 
lessons unfold. 
GAMEPLAN is the result of a combined total of over 50 years of teaching music to 
children. During that time many teachers, colleagues, friends, and family influenced our 
work; however, the greatest impact on this project came from our students. For all of the 
joy, fascination, frustration, and wonder they provide, GAMERAN is dedicated to those 
children . . . our greatest teachers.

Kindergarten students are full of energy. They need a variety of activities with numerous 
opportunities for spontaneous play. They require an abundance of physical activity, so 
movement is essential. Being still is often more stressful for children of this age than moving. 
Exploration and investigation help nurture intellectual growth. 
GAMEPLAN includes one lesson per week with approximately 60 minutes of activities. 
Each of the lessons is planned sequentially, addressing objectives through a variety of 
experiences in different stages of learning. Many considerations were made when planning 
the lessons; however, most teachers do not instruct students once a week for an hour. 
Consequently, as you adapt the lessons to your own teaching circumstances, keep in mind 
the following: 
• kindergarten students need a wide variety of activities in each lesson (if you 
see students more than once a week, consider introducing the activities 
during the first lesson and reviewing or extending them during the second) 
• if you instruct students less than 60 minutes per week, consider the stage of 
teaching for each activity (introduce, reinforce, assess) when determining 
which ones to include 
• most teachers will not have all of the instruments and materials needed to teach 
every lesson, but do not be discouraged . . . building a music program is an 
ongoing process and requires flexibility and a willingness to adapt 
In every lesson: 
• state the objective(s) at the beginning and review the concepts at the end 
• transition from activity to activity quickly 
• develop ways to "re-focus" the students during an activity; for example: 
stand/sit, reposition your placement in the room, introduce a visual, etc. 
• include movement . . in everylesson 
• develop questioning techniques that require higher-level thinking skills 
• see the experience through the students' eyes. . . What is special? What will 
they enjoy? What will they share with their parents when they get home? 
Unless you are a beginning teacher, you probably have activities you have successfully 
experienced with your kindergarten students. If you would like to incorporate them into 
GAMEPLAN: 
• identify which objective(s) the activity hest supports (be certain the teaching 
process teaches to the objective) 
• determine whether the activity best introduces, reinforces, or assesses the 
objective (this will help you know when to teach the activity) 
• consult the "Year At A Glance" chart on page viii to know when to substitute 
or add the activity 
The National Standards for Arts Education - Music Content Standards: 
1. Singing, alone and with others, a varied repertoire of music 
2. Performing on instruments, alone and with others, a varied repertoire of music 
3. Improvising melodies, variations, and accompaniments 
4. Composing and arranging music within specified guidelines 
5. Reading and notating music 
6. Listening to, analyzing, and describing music 
7. Evaluating music and music performances 
8. Understanding relationships between music, the other arts, and disciplines outside the arts 
9. Understanding music in relation to history and culture

Duck, Duck, Goose 
Introduce or review this popular children's game. In a seated circle, one 
student (leader) walks around the outside of the circle tapping each student's

around the circle. The chaser becomes the new leader.

This is an activity where students provide individual responses that are 
repeated in a sequence of four. The song above frames the activity which 
alternates between singing and individual responses. 
In a standing circle, sing song (students will join when comfortable) followed 
by the first student pantomiming something a boatman might do (swab deck, 
fish, dance, clap hands, etc.). After the song, follow this sequence: 
• First student pantomimes action and class copies. 
• Second student pantomimes a new action (class copies), 
followed by all repeating first student's action. 
• Third student pantomimes a new action (class copies), 
followed by second, then first student's action. 
• Fourth student pantomimes a new action (class copies), 
followed by third, second, then first student's action. 
Sing song again, repeating activity with next four students. Continue until all 
have had a turn. 
Sequencing is an important skill in kindergarten, 
and this activity sequences in reverse. Plan 10-12

In a standing circle with hands joined and raised to create arches, teacher 
demonstrates movement weaving in and out of the windows while singing 
song. At the end of the song the teacher taps someone to be the new leader

the tonal center changes. Minor scales are based on La.
